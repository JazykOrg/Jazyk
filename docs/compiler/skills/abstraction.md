Abstraction is the graph's answer to scale. Downward: an entity over its requirement limit (50 soft, 80 hard) or a subject over its state limit (12, 20) is split into sub-entities under it, detail moved down, and the documents asked to state the new structure. Upward: a level (a node's direct children, or a scope's parentless entities) over its children limit (9, 15) is grouped under the node, the fan-out change. Lifting keeps every coarse view true, and each level gets its own diagrams with drill-down. This skill applies in every medium: a service split into modules, a department into teams, a long chapter's cast into scenes, a deck's section into slides.

Read everything first. A GC goal runs only once the cone is quiet, so the counts you see are final for this build: load the entity in full and every requirement on it across all documents for a split, or the target with its children as stubs and the relationships among them for a fan-out, expanding a member only when a boundary needs it. Abstract once, holistically, never in a stream of partial states.

Groupings. A grouping is a concept a reader of the documents would recognize and name, never a coupling artifact. The harness computes coupling and never names; you name and judge and never count.
- The change's candidates are partitions over the children's relationships: accept, adjust with a reason (a candidate mixing two responsibilities splits; two the documents treat as one area merge), or decline.
- Document names and headings are the strongest naming hints (a `payment.md` suggests a Payments grouping); prefer a name the documents already use.
- Search before you create: a grouping's name is judged like an entity name, and a lookalike of an existing area reuses it. A child that belongs under an existing sibling moves there with `update_entity` `parent`, with the reason.
- A stated entity that carries the area's name is the area's node, never a collision: when a document headed Checkout states a Checkout beside the Cart, the Coupon, and the Receipt, the Checkout is the level's node and the others move under it (the `namesake` hint names it). A node is both a thing and the frame of the level below it, the way a service is a component and the parent of its modules; refusing to nest a process's parts under the process because "a process is not an area" invents a distinction the documents do not draw, and minting a twin named after it is what the near-duplicate gate refuses.
- A split the documents do not suggest (model, view, controller) is a choice: say so in the reasoning; ratification carries it to the owner.
- A grouping never crosses levels (its members share one parent, which it takes) or scopes; its stereotype is from the existing vocabulary or absent.
- Stage each with `group_entities` (`name`, `definition`, `members`, `stereotype` when one fits, `reasoning`): one derived entity under the node, the members moved under it, one changeset. Higher levels carry no requirements; lifting covers edges and flows.
- The definition is one sentence stating the grouping's responsibility, as prose for the document that owns the node (the front door at the top level); the ratification proposal carries it.
- A grouping that lost its reason dissolves with `dissolve_entity`, never an entity a document states; under two children the sweep dissolves it.
- Declining is honest work: a genuinely flat level (nine peers with no cohesion) fails the goal with that reason, never invented tiers.

Split by cohesion.
- Group the requirements by what they are about: a sub-concept, a phase, a role, an interface, a lifecycle stage, a recurring noun. A group is a candidate sub-entity when the documents already have a noun for it (a section title, a repeated phrase, a list item) and its statements are about that noun directly.
- Name the sub-entity in the documents' own wording. Never invent a concept the documents cannot support: a group with no noun in the documents is not a sub-entity. When the requirements cohere into no such groups, fail the goal with the reason and let the human decide.
- Respect scopes: a split never crosses a scope. Respect stated containment: where the documents state a whole-part (a `composition` edge), the part's `parent` is that whole, and no sub-entity may contradict it.
- Search before you create. A noun the documents have may already be an entity elsewhere in the tree; a sub-entity that exists is re-parented with `update_entity` `parent`, never minted twice. Two same-named entities under different parents are two concepts, so always pass `parent`.

Build the tree.
- Create each sub-entity with `upsert_entity`, passing `provenance: {derived: {from, reasoning}}` in place of `mention`, since no sentence states it yet: `parent` the entity, `definition` written as the sentence the documents should gain, in their vocabulary, a `stereotype` when the medium calls for one, `from` the requirements moved under it in this session, and `reasoning` the cohesion they follow. The registry refuses an entity with neither `mention` nor `provenance`.
- Move the detail: re-point each grouped requirement with `update_requirement`, passing only `id` and `entities`, the sub-entity in place of the parent when the statement is about the part, the parent kept when the statement is about the whole. Keep `transition.subject` and every edge end in `entities`. Attributes move with their concept, through `update_entity` on the sub-entity.
- The parent keeps the statements about the whole and its definition; refresh the definition to name the parts when the documents' wording does.
- Over the children limit, group the level as above; never split the node.
- Over the state limit, the subject conflates phases or concepts. Split the subject into a sub-entity per phase whose states are that phase's, and re-point each transition statement's `entities` and `transition.subject` to the sub-entity whose states it uses. Never merge states to lower the count.

Docs proposals. Every sub-entity or grouping with derived provenance is invented until the documents state it. The harness files a ratification proposal per derived fact: the sentence the docs should gain, inserted into the best section, accepted or retracted by a human through the `ratify` goal.
- Your part is the sentence. The `definition` is what the proposal carries, so write it as prose the author would keep, in the documents' vocabulary ("The order service consists of the cart module, the pricing module, and the fulfillment module."), never as graph jargon.
- Prefer a target the documents already structure: the section that introduces the parent, or a new sub-document when the parent's section is already over its size limit. Name the target in the note.
- One proposal per fact, never a bundle: a parent that gains three parts is three sentences, or one sentence naming the three, and the note says which.

Views. After a split or a grouping, the views that showed the entity keep drawing it: lifting folds the descendants' relationships into the nearest shown ancestor, and a level view derives at commit for every node with two or more children. Collapse the parent in the coarse views that do not need the internals; give the internals a sub-view when they earn one.

Limits and dismissal. When the count is honest and every split or grouping would invent structure, do not abstract. Raising the node's own limit is a human's decree, recorded with decree provenance, and the goal stops deriving until the raised threshold is crossed. Fail the goal with that recommendation in the reason; the failure surfaces on the target where the human will see it.

Docs split versus graph split. The same pressure can be answered by splitting a section or splitting an entity. A section over its size limit is the author's to split (`section-too-large`); the graph split is yours only when the requirements cohere into sub-concepts the documents name. When the pressure comes from one oversized section that lists everything about one concept, leave the entity whole and fail the goal pointing at the section.

Retrace. When a sub-entity's parent died, re-point `parent` to the nearest live ancestor with `update_entity`, or unset it when none remains; when a merge absorbed the parent, the redirect target is the new parent. When a derived entity's `from` set lost a member, re-derive it: keep it when the surviving requirements still cohere under its noun, saying why; when they do not, move its requirements back to the parent with `update_requirement` and delete it with a reason. A dead node is never recreated here.

Stability. A split or grouping re-derived on a later build must land on the same natural keys (the same names under the same parent), so the upserts are no-ops. Never re-split what a compile review merged back: a flip between a GC split and a compile merge parks the pair as `unstable-derivation`, blocked on a human. A child moved back between the same two parents is a reparent flip and parks alike. Never delete the parent, and never move a requirement whose statement is about the whole.

Justify in one or two sentences: for a split, the cohesion each sub-entity follows and the sentence in the documents that names it; for a grouping, the groupings and why the domain would recognize them.
