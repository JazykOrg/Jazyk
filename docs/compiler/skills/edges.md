Edges. When a statement ties two entities structurally, declare the pair in `edges`, directional (`a` acts on `b`) and typed: the whole is `a` of a `composition` or an `aggregation` (a belongs-to sentence makes the container `a`), the realizer `a` of a `realization`, the instance `a` of an `instantiation`, the dependent `a` of a `dependency`, the specific `a` of a `generalization`; `association` for a plain link, `aggregation` for a shared part. Composition claims exclusive ownership: one whole, and the part's `parent` follows it. When the part is shared across wholes or listed by several ("Orders contain Products from the Catalog"), the type is `aggregation`. Always state the type: an edge without one counts as the weakest, `dependency`. Add `cardinality` (`1`, `0..1`, `1..*`, `*`) when the sentence states a count, as the multiplicity at `b`, how many `b` per `a` ("holds one or more order items" is `1..*` on the items; "every employee belongs to exactly one department" with the Department as `a` is a count of departments per employee, which the `a` to `b` direction cannot say, so it stays in the statement). One sentence, several arrows: "A calls B's endpoint" yields `A→endpoint` and `A→B`, both `dependency`. Both ends of every edge are in `entities`. A sub-system list is the common case: each part ties to its whole.
- When a goal asks for edges on a multi-entity statement that has none, declare them when the statement ties the entities structurally. When it only names them in passing ("the report lists orders and customers"), declare none and say in the justification that the statement is not structural.
- A condition that names another entity's state is context, never structure: "A confirmation email is not sent if the Product is out of stock" constrains the Shop's behavior and states no whole-part, use, or link between Shop and Product, so it carries no edge; the same holds for a trigger ("when the Payment is confirmed, the Order is marked paid" ties nothing, it records a transition on the Order). An edge drawn from a condition puts a false arrow, and a false message, in every view that shows the pair.

Stability. Prefer a no-op over cosmetic rewording; the graph's stability across builds matters more than polish. A statement already extracted and still made by the section is left alone. Reword an existing requirement with `update_requirement` on its id, never with a second upsert. A call that only adds entities or edges to a requirement passes only `id` and that field, never `section` or `quote`, which re-anchor the provenance.
