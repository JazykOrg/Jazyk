# The Lantern Room

This is the bible for *The Lantern Room*, a short romance novel set in the harbor town of
Port Alder. Everything the manuscript has to honor is written down here: who the
characters are, where the story happens, what happens in each chapter, how the two leads
change, and how the prose is written. The manuscript is the deliverable. This bible is
the source it is built from, and when the two disagree, the bible is what gets fixed
first.

## Premise

Port Alder's harbor wall is coming down. Pryce Maritime has bought the quay and plans to
replace the wall, and the row of shops standing on it, with a marina. Ines Marlow runs
the Lantern Room, a bookshop on that row that her family has kept for three generations.
Theo Brandt is the marine surveyor Pryce Maritime hires to survey the wall before the
demolition. He rents the room above the shop without saying who sent him.

The novel runs one autumn, from Theo's arrival on the ferry to the night of the storm
and the week after it. Ines and Theo fall in love over the fight for the wall; the
[arcs](./arcs.md) say how, one stance at a time. The wall stays up. The marina is never
built.

## The deliverable

The deliverable is the manuscript. The manuscript is a set of chapters, one markdown
file per chapter, in a directory called `manuscript/` beside this bible. The files are
numbered in reading order and named after the chapter title:

```
manuscript/01-arrival.md
manuscript/02-the-survey.md
manuscript/03-the-ledger.md
manuscript/04-the-quay.md
manuscript/05-the-council.md
manuscript/06-the-storm.md
manuscript/07-the-notebook.md
manuscript/08-the-lantern-room.md
```

The manuscript has eight chapters. Each chapter file begins with the chapter title as a
level-one heading and contains nothing but the chapter's prose after it: no notes, no
front matter, no scene labels.

Every chapter is a run of scenes. A scene has a point of view, a setting, a goal, and an
outcome. The point of view is the character whose head the scene stays in. The setting
is one of the places in [settings](./settings.md). The goal is what the point-of-view
character wants when the scene opens, and the outcome is what they have when it closes.
A scene never changes its point of view partway through. The [plot](./plot.md) lists
every scene and writes one of them out in full as a worked example.

## Continuity

Names are spelled one way in every chapter. Ines takes no accent. Port Alder is two
words. The Lantern Room is capitalized as a proper name, and the lantern in its window
is not. Theo's surname is Brandt with a t. Chapter titles in the manuscript match the
chapter titles in the plot.
