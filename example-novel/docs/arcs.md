# Arcs

Each lead moves through the same five stances toward the other: stranger, acquaintance,
rival, ally, beloved. Ines's stance is toward Theo and Theo's stance is toward Ines. A
stance changes only when something happens on the page, so every trigger below is a
scene in the [plot](./plot.md). Neither lead ever moves back down a stance.

Both leads start as strangers. Both end as beloved, and beloved is where the book leaves
them: nothing moves either lead out of it.

## Ines

Ines is 31 and has never had a reason to leave Port Alder. The arc gives her one, in the
shape of Theo's next contract, and she declines it. Her stance toward Theo changes four
times:

| From | To | Trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stranger | acquaintance | Theo takes the upstairs room |
| acquaintance | rival | Dot tells Ines who Theo works for |
| rival | ally | Theo offers Ines the crack finding |
| ally | beloved | Theo shores up the wall through the storm |

The rival stretch is the long one, from chapter 3 to chapter 5, and it is where Ines is
wrong about Theo for reasons that are entirely his fault.

## Theo

Theo is 36 when he arrives and has been moving from job to job for twelve years. The arc
stops him. His stance toward Ines changes four times:

| From | To | Trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stranger | acquaintance | Theo finds the crack beneath the Lantern Room |
| acquaintance | rival | Ines confronts Theo about the survey |
| rival | ally | Ines's plan fails at the council |
| ally | beloved | Ines asks Theo to stay |

Theo's first change happens before he has said ten words to Ines: finding the crack
under her shop makes her real to him before she is anything else.

## The midpoint

The midpoint is chapter 4, the confrontation on the quay. It is the scene the first half
points at and the second half looks back on, and it has to land both leads at once. When
Ines confronts Theo about the survey, Theo moves from acquaintance to ally. He has
already decided, on the wall at low tide, that she should know about the crack, and the
confrontation gives him the opening he was waiting for. Ines does not know this yet,
which is what makes chapter 5 hurt: she walks into the council believing him her enemy.

## Rules for the arcs

- A stance change is shown in a scene, never narrated in summary.
- Ines and Theo never change stance in the same scene.
- Each stance change is visible to the reader before the lead admits it to themselves.
