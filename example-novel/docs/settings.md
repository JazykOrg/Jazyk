# Settings

The whole book happens in Port Alder. Three places matter; everything else is a street
the characters walk along between them.

## Port Alder

Port Alder is a harbor town on the west coast with about four thousand residents. The
town climbs a hill above a stone harbor wall, and the quay runs along the top of the
wall. The ferry is the only way in that does not mean a two-hour drive around the
estuary. In autumn the ferry crosses four times a day, and it does not run after dark.

The harbor wall was built in 1840 and was last surveyed in 1988. The quay carries a row
of six shops, and the Lantern Room is the third from the ferry office. The harbor
council owns the wall and leases the quay frontage to the shops standing on it.

## The Lantern Room

The Lantern Room is a bookshop on the quay in Port Alder. It has two floors: the shop on
the ground floor and a single room above it, which Ines lets to lodgers when she needs
the money. The shop is named for the ship's lantern that hangs in the front window, and
the lantern is lit every evening at dusk.

The shop opens at nine and closes at six, and it does not open on Sundays. The takings
ledger lives under the counter, where Ines writes the day's takings in pencil. The
harbor ledger does not live in the shop: it lives in the ferry office safe, and the two
ledgers are never confused on the page.

The upstairs room has one window, facing the harbor, and the crack Theo finds runs
directly beneath it.

## Marlow House

Marlow House is the Marlow family's house on Chapel Lane in Port Alder, up the hill from
the harbor. Ines lives there alone. The house has been in the family as long as the shop
lease, and the two are spoken of together. No scene of the novel is set inside Marlow
House: it is seen from the quay and mentioned, never entered.

## Research notes

Things I looked up while outlining, kept here so I do not look them up twice. A harbor
wall survey has three parts, a walkover, core samples, and a written report. Surveyors
write the report from the field notebook rather than from memory, which is why the
notebook carries the plot. A written survey report takes three days to produce, so
Theo's report cannot exist before chapter 3. West coast ferries publish their autumn
timetables in late August. A stone bookshop in October holds a temperature of about
twelve degrees.
