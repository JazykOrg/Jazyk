# Style

How the prose is written. These are rules for the manuscript, not suggestions; a chapter
that breaks one is not finished.

## Tense and point of view

The manuscript is written in the past tense. The point of view is close third person:
the narration stays inside one character's head per scene and reports only what that
character sees, hears, and thinks. The narrator never comments from outside the scene.

Point of view alternates between Ines and Theo chapter by chapter. Odd chapters belong
to Ines and even chapters belong to Theo, so chapter 1 opens in Ines's head watching the
ferry come in and chapter 8 closes in Theo's. Within a chapter every scene keeps the
chapter's point of view; a scene that needs the other lead's head goes in the other
lead's chapter.

Callum and Dot never carry the point of view. What they think is shown only through what
Ines or Theo sees them do.

## Length

Each chapter runs between 2,000 and 3,500 words. The whole manuscript runs under 24,000
words. A chapter that comes in short is padded with nothing: it is cut from the outline
instead, and the plot page is changed to match.

## Prose rules

- Dialogue uses double quotation marks and a new paragraph per speaker.
- No scene opens on the weather, except the first scene of chapter 6, which opens on
  the storm warning.
- The word "love" is not said aloud by either lead before chapter 8.
- Theo's measurements are given in metres and centimetres, never feet, because that is
  how he writes them in the notebook.
- Ines never calls the shop "the bookshop"; to her it is the shop or the Lantern Room.
- Nobody explains the lease clause in dialogue; the reader gets it when Ines reads it,
  in chapter 3, from the page.

## Inspirations

None of this is a rule, and nothing in the manuscript is checked against it. These are
the books and films I reread while outlining, listed so I remember where I was standing
when I started, not so anyone matches them:

- *Persuasion*, Jane Austen.
- *The Shipping News*, Annie Proulx.
- *Local Hero*, the 1983 film.
- *84, Charing Cross Road*, Helene Hanff.
- *The Lighthouse Stevensons*, Bella Bathurst.
