# The survey

Theo Brandt had done a hundred harbor wall surveys, and every one of them ran the same three parts.

The first was the walkover. He went down to the wall at low tide and walked its length, reading the masonry with his hands and his eyes, looking for the places where the stone had shifted or the mortar had let go. A walkover told you where to look harder.

The second was the core samples. Where the walkover raised a question, Theo drove a tube into the wall and pulled out a core, a column of the wall's own stone and mortar, and read it the way a doctor reads a blood test. The core samples were the part of the survey that could not be faked; the wall told the truth in the tube, whether or not anyone wanted to hear it.

The third was the written report. From the walkover and the core samples, and from the field notebook he kept between them, Theo wrote the report that the council would read. The report was the part that decided what happened to the wall.

Three parts, then: a walkover, core samples, and a written report. That was the whole of a harbor wall survey, and Theo had never known one that ran any other way.
