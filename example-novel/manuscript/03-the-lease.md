# The lease

The lease of the Lantern Room was the thing the family kept, the way other families keep a photograph or a ring. It was a folded paper in a tin on the top shelf of the back room, and Ines had seen it twice in her life, both times when she was small and both times when her mother was not there to explain it.

The Marlow family had held the lease for three generations. Ines's grandmother had signed it in 1961, the year the harbor wall was rebuilt after the winter that took the old one, and the paper had passed from her to her daughter, and from her daughter to Ines, the way a key passes, without ceremony and without question. The lease granted the Marlows the quay frontage for as long as the harbor wall stood, and the wall had stood, and so the lease had held.

Ines's grandmother was the first of the three. She had signed the paper and she had kept the room and she had died in it, and the family spoke of her the way the harbor speaks of a tide that has gone out: as a thing that was there and is not, and that the water remembers. No one in the book of the room had seen her, and no one who came after would. She was a name on the lease and a voice in the stories, and that was the whole of her.

Ines's mother was the second of the three, and she was the one Ines could not picture. The mother had kept the room between the grandmother and Ines, and she had kept it well, and she had gone away from it the way a tide goes out, and the family spoke of her in the same careful way they spoke of the grandmother, as a thing that was there and is not. Ines had been a child when the mother left, and a child does not keep a face, and so the mother was a name and a memory of a name, and no one who came after would see her either.

Three generations, then: the grandmother who signed, the mother who kept, and Ines who held. The first two were spoken of and never seen, and Ines was the only Marlow who stood in the room and lit the lantern and kept the kettle on, and that was the shape of the family, and the shape of the lease, and the shape of the room.
