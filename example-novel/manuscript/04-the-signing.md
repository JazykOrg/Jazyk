# The signing

The lease of the Lantern Room was a thing that began in 1961, and it began with a signature.

Ines's grandmother had signed it in 1961, the year the harbor wall was rebuilt after the winter that took the old one. She had gone to the council with the paper and the pen, and she had put her name to the bottom of it, and the council had put theirs to the top, and the lease had begun. The lease granted the Marlows the quay frontage for as long as the harbor wall stood, and the wall had stood, and so the lease had held.

The family had held the lease for three generations since that signing. The grandmother had held it first, and she had kept the room and she had died in it. Her daughter had held it second, and she had kept the room and she had gone away from it. Ines had held it third, and she kept the room still, and she lit the lantern and she kept the kettle on, and the lease held in her hands the way it had held in the hands of the two before her.

Three generations, then, since the signing in 1961: the grandmother who signed, the mother who kept, and Ines who held. The lease was the thread that ran through the three, and the thread had not broken, and the family had held the lease of the Lantern Room for three generations, since Ines's grandmother signed it in 1961.
