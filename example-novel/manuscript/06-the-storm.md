# The storm

The storm came in off the north by midnight, and by the time it found the harbor wall it had been building for two days.

Theo Brandt was already at the crack when the first real gust hit. He had not slept, and he did not mean to. The crack ran the length of the wall's north face, a seam of failed mortar that the core samples had told him was repairable, and the storm was the test of whether it would hold.

The harbor crew came to him in pairs as the tide turned. There were six of them, the men and women who worked the wall and the boats, and they brought the timber, the wedges, the tarred rope, and the lanterns. Theo set the first brace across the crack and the crew drove the wedges behind it, and then the second brace, and then the third, each one a little lower, each one taking a little more of the load the storm was trying to put on the stone.

They worked through the night. Theo called the braces and the crew set them, and when a brace would not take he called for another, and they brought it, and they drove it home. The crack was the work, and the work was the crack, and the storm was the clock that would tell them whether they had enough time.

By the time the wind dropped to a moan, the north face was braced from top to bottom, and the crack held.

Ines Marlow kept the Lantern Room open for them. She had lit the lantern before the storm and she did not let it go out, and she kept the kettle on the stove so that when a man or a woman came in from the wall with cold hands and a soaked coat, there was light and there was hot water and there was a chair by the fire. She kept the lantern lit and the kettle on for the crew until dawn, and when the first light came over the water the crew came in one by one, and Ines was still there, and the lantern was still lit, and the kettle was still on.
