# The notebook

The council met on a Thursday, and Callum Pryce came to it with the notebook in his hand.

The notebook was the ledger of the harbor, the book in which the wall and the lease and the frontage were written down, and Callum had brought it to the table because he had something to say about it. He set it open on the wood, and the council looked at the open page, and the open page was not the page they had expected.

"Cut the lease page out of the ledger," Callum said, and he said it to the council, and he said it to the room, and he did not look away from it. "I cut the lease page out of the ledger. I did it myself, and I did it on purpose, and I will not say why except that I did it."

The council was quiet for a moment, and then the quiet was the kind that follows a thing that has been said and cannot be unsaid. Callum had admitted it, the cutting of the lease page, and the admission was the thing that stood in the room.

Then Callum closed the notebook, and he set it down, and he said the other thing. "I withdraw the marina plan," he said. "The plan I brought to you, the plan for the marina, I withdraw it now. It is not before you, and it is not mine to put before you again, and I take it back."

The withdrawal was the second thing, and it followed the first, the way a hand follows a hand, and the council saw it, and the reader saw it: Callum had admitted the cutting, and then Callum had withdrawn the marina plan, and the two stood together in the room, the admission and the withdrawal, and the marina plan was gone from the table the way the lease page was gone from the ledger.

Callum did not say more. He closed the notebook, and he left the room, and the council was left with the open ledger and the missing page and the plan that was no longer before them, and the harbor was the harbor still, and the wall was the wall, and the lease was the lease, and the marina plan was the thing that had been withdrawn.
