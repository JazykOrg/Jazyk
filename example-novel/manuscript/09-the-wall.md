# The wall

Port Alder's harbor wall was coming down. That was the thing the whole town was talking about, the thing that had come to sit on every table and in every doorway, the thing that the ferry and the shops and the houses on the hill all looked out toward. The wall had stood for a hundred and fifty years, and now it was to come down, and the town could not say why except that it was the plan, and the plan was the thing that had been bought.

Pryce Maritime had bought the quay. That was the first of it, and the buying was the thing that made the rest of it possible. Pryce Maritime had bought the quay and planned to replace the wall, and the row of shops standing on it, with a marina. The marina was the plan, and the plan was the thing that would stand where the wall had stood, and where the row of shops had stood, and where the Lantern Room had stood. The marina was never built. The wall stayed up, and the row of shops stayed on the quay, and the marina was the thing that was planned and not built, the plan that the town had talked about and then had not.

Theo Brandt was the marine surveyor Pryce Maritime hired to survey the wall before the demolition. He was the man they had sent, the man who would walk the wall and read it and write the report, and the report was the thing that would tell the council whether the wall could be repaired or had to come down. Theo was the surveyor, and the survey was the thing that the demolition waited on, and the demolition was the thing the marina waited on.

Ines and Theo fell in love over the fight for the wall. It was not a thing that happened at once, and it was not a thing that happened by accident. It happened over the fight, the fight for the wall, the fight that the two of them were in together, and the fight was the thing that brought them to each other, and the wall was the thing they were both fighting for, and the loving was the thing that came out of the fighting, the way a tide comes out of the sea.

The wall stayed up. That was the end of it, and the end was the thing the whole book was working toward. The wall had been coming down, and the plan had been to take it down, and the marina had been the thing that would stand in its place, and then the wall stayed up, and the marina was not built, and the row of shops stayed on the quay, and the Lantern Room stayed in its place, and the wall was the wall still, the wall that had stood for a hundred and fifty years and would stand for a hundred and fifty more.

Port Alder climbed a hill above a stone harbor wall, and the quay ran along the top of the wall. From the harbor you looked up at the town, and from the town you looked down at the harbor, and the wall was the line between the two, the stone line that the quay ran along the top of, and the shops stood on the quay, and the houses stood on the hill, and the wall was the thing that held the harbor back from the town and the town back from the harbor.

The harbor wall had been built in 1840, and it had been last surveyed in 1988. Those were the two years the town kept for the wall, the year it was built and the year it was last read, and the hundred and forty-eight years between them were the years the wall had stood without a survey, the years the stone had held on its own, and the 1840 was the year the wall began and the 1988 was the year the last surveyor had read it, and after that the wall had stood on its own until Theo came to read it again.

The harbor council owned the wall, and the council leased the quay frontage to the shops standing on it. The council was the owner, and the shops were the tenants, and the frontage was the thing the council let the shops stand on, and the lease was the paper that said so, and the wall was the thing the council owned and the frontage was the thing the council leased, and the shops stood on the frontage the way the frontage stood on the wall.

Callum had hired Theo to survey the harbor wall before the demolition. Callum was the project lead at Pryce Maritime, and the marina was his first named project, and the survey was the thing the marina needed, and the survey was the thing Theo was hired for. Callum hired Theo, and Theo was the man who would walk the wall and read it and write the report, and the report was the thing that would tell the council whether the wall could be repaired or had to come down, and the demolition was the thing the marina waited on.

The lease granted the Marlows the quay frontage for as long as the harbor wall stood, and that was why the lease mattered. The frontage was the family's, and the family held it for as long as the wall held, and the wall was the clock that would tell them when the frontage was gone. The lease was the paper that said the frontage was the Marlows' for as long as the wall stood, and the wall was the thing the lease was tied to, and the frontage was the thing the lease granted, and the matter of the lease was the matter of the wall, the two of them held together the way a key and a lock are held together.

Theo walked the harbor wall at low tide, and he found a crack in the footing directly beneath the Lantern Room. He went down to the wall when the tide was out, and he walked its length, reading the masonry with his hands and his eyes, and at the place beneath the Lantern Room he found the crack, a seam in the footing, a place where the stone had let go and the mortar had failed, and the crack ran the length of the footing beneath the room, and the room was the Lantern Room, and the crack was the thing the wall was failing at.

On the wall at low tide, Theo had already decided that Ines should know about the crack. He had found it, and he had read it, and he had written it down in the field notebook, and then he had stood at the crack and looked up at the Lantern Room, and he had decided that she should know, that the woman who kept the room should know that the wall beneath it was failing, and the decision was the thing he had made on the wall at low tide, and the confrontation that came after was the opening he had been waiting for.

Callum told Theo to leave the crack out of the written report, because a wall that could be repaired was a wall the council would not demolish. Callum had read the report, and he had seen the crack, and he had told Theo to leave it out, to take the crack out of the report, because a wall that could be repaired was a wall the council would not take down, and the marina needed the wall down, and the crack was the thing that would keep the wall up, and so the crack was the thing that had to go out of the report.

Ines found the Marlow lease in the harbor ledger, and she read the clause that granted the frontage for as long as the wall stood. She had gone to the ferry office and asked Dot for the harbor ledger, the book that recorded every lease on the quay, and she had found the Marlow lease in it, and she had read the clause, the clause that granted the Marlows the quay frontage for as long as the harbor wall stood, and the clause was the thing that made the lease matter, and the wall was the thing the lease was tied to.

Ines decided to take the lease to the harbor council and block the demolition. She had read the clause, and she had understood it, and she had decided that the lease was the thing that would stop the wall coming down, that the council would not take down a wall that the lease tied to the frontage, and so she decided to take the lease to the council, to put it before them, to block the demolition with the paper that said the frontage was the Marlows' for as long as the wall stood.

Ines confronted Theo on the quay about the survey, and she asked him what he had found. She had come to the quay, and she had found him there, and she had asked him about the survey, the survey of the wall, and she had asked him what he had found, and the asking was the thing that brought the crack out, the crack that he had found and that he had been told to leave out of the report, and the confrontation was the thing that gave him the opening he had been waiting for.

Ines presented the harbor ledger to the harbor council to block the demolition. She had brought the ledger to the meeting, and she had set it open on the table, and she had presented it to the council, the book that recorded every lease on the quay, and she had presented it to block the demolition, to stop the wall coming down, to put the lease before the council and let the council read the clause that tied the frontage to the wall.

The council voted to proceed with demolishing the harbor wall. The plan had failed, because when Ines opened the ledger at the meeting, the lease page had been cut out, and the page was gone, and the council could not read the clause that was not there, and so the council voted to proceed with the demolition, the vote that would take the wall down, the vote that the missing page had made possible.

Theo offered Ines the crack finding as proof that the wall could be repaired instead of replaced. He had the finding, the crack he had found and written down, and he had offered it to Ines, the finding that the wall could be repaired, that the crack was a thing that could be fixed, and the offering was the thing that gave Ines the proof, the proof that the wall did not have to come down, that it could be repaired instead of replaced.

Theo shored up the crack with the harbor crew through the night of the storm. The storm came in off the north, and Theo was at the crack when it hit, and the harbor crew came to him in pairs, and they brought the timber and the wedges and the tarred rope, and they worked through the night, and the crack held, and the wall held, and the storm was the test and the wall passed it.

When Theo shored up the wall through the storm, Ines's stance toward Theo changed from ally to beloved. They had been allies, the two of them, fighting for the wall together, and then the storm came, and Theo shored up the crack through the night, and Ines kept the lantern lit and the kettle on for the crew until dawn, and in the doing of it, in the night of the storm and the dawn after, her stance toward him changed, the ally became the beloved, and the loving was the thing that came out of the storm, the way a tide comes out of the sea.

Ines gave Theo's field notebook to the harbor council as evidence that the crack was known and left out of the report. She had found the notebook in the upstairs room, and she had read the measurements he had written down twice, and she had given it to the council, the notebook that showed the crack had been found and known and then left out of the report, and the notebook was the evidence, the evidence that the crack was known and omitted, and the council read it, and the reading was the thing that turned the vote.
