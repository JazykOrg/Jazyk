# Callum Pryce

Callum Pryce was thirty-three years old, and he was the project lead at Pryce Maritime, his father's company. The company was the thing his father had built and the thing Callum now ran, and the project lead was the title that sat on the quay the way the company sat on the town, and the thirty-three years were the thing that made him the man who stood over the marina and not only a man who worked for a company on the quay.

The marina was the first project with his name on it. Before the marina, the projects had carried his father's name, and the marina was the first one that carried Callum's, and the marina was the thing that would stand where the wall and the row of shops stood, and the name on the marina was the name that made Callum the project lead and not only the son of the man who had built the company.

Callum believed the marina would save the town, and the book let him believe it. He believed it the way a man believes the thing that will save the thing he loves, and the marina was the thing he had set his name to, and the town was the thing the marina would save, and the belief was the thing that drove him through the autumn, and the book let him hold it, the book that did not take it from him and did not give it to anyone else.

He was not a villain, and he was not written as one. He was the man who believed the marina would save the town, and he was the man who did the thing that was not the thing a villain would do, and the thing he did was the thing that came from the belief and not from the malice, and the book wrote him as the man who was not a villain and who was not written as one, the man who did the thing and answered for it and was not the thing the thing was not.

He cut the lease page out of the harbor ledger. The lease page was the page that held the clause that tied the frontage to the wall, and the harbor ledger was the book that Dot kept safe in the ferry office, and Callum cut the page out of it, and the cutting was the thing he did, and the page was the thing he cut, and the ledger was the thing he cut it from, and the cutting was the thing that was not the thing a villain would do and that was the thing he answered for.

In chapter 7, Callum answered for cutting the lease page out of the harbor ledger. The answering was the thing that came after the cutting, and chapter 7 was the chapter in which the answering happened, and the answering was the thing that made him accountable for the cutting, the thing that turned the man who had cut the page into the man who stood before the council and said the thing he had done.

Ines and Callum knew each other from school. They had gone to school together, and they had been close until they were nineteen, and the school was the place the two of them had known each other, and the closeness was the thing that had run from the school to the nineteen, and the nineteen was the age at which the closeness had ended, and the knowing was the thing that had come from the school and that the two of them carried into the town.

Dot had called Ines Nessa since Ines was six, and Callum had picked the name up at school. The name had come to Dot the way a name comes to a person who has watched a child grow, and Callum had picked it up at the school he and Ines had gone to together, and the name had passed from Dot to Callum the way a name passes between the people who know a child, and the Nessa was the name the two of them used and no one else did.

Nobody else used the name. Dot used it, and Callum used it, and no one else in the town used it, and Ines did not answer to it from strangers, because the name was the name of the people who had known her since she was small, and a stranger who called her Nessa was a stranger who did not know her, and Ines did not answer to a name that was not hers from the people who had given it to her.

Callum and Theo knew each other only through the contract. Before the contract, the two of them had not known each other, and the contract was the thing that brought them together, and the contract was the thing that made Callum the one who hired Theo and Theo the one who was hired, and the knowing was the thing that came only through the contract and not through the school and not through the town.

Callum hired Theo to survey the harbor wall before the demolition. Theo worked freelance for whoever was paying, and this autumn Pryce Maritime was paying, and Callum was the one who hired him, and the hiring was the thing that brought Theo to the quay, and the survey was the thing Theo was hired for, and the demolition was the thing the survey came before, and the harbor wall was the thing the survey was of.

In chapter 2, Callum told Theo to leave the crack out of the written report. The crack was the thing Theo had found in the footing beneath the Lantern Room, and the written report was the part of the survey that the council would read, and Callum told Theo to leave the crack out of it, because a wall that could be repaired was a wall the council would not demolish, and the telling was the thing that came from Callum and was directed at Theo, and the leaving out was the thing that would keep the wall standing in the council's eyes.

In chapter 4, Theo refused to show Ines the report and told her to ask Callum. Ines came to the quay and asked for the report, the written report of the survey, and Theo refused to show it to her, and the refusal was the thing that came from Theo and was directed at Ines, and Theo told her to ask Callum, and the asking was the thing Theo put in her place, and the report was the thing she was not shown.

In chapter 5, Callum asked Nessa to drop the petition, for old times' sake. The petition was the thing Ines had set up against the demolition, and Callum asked her to drop it, and the asking was the thing that came from Callum and was directed at Nessa, and the old times were the thing he appealed to, the school and the closeness that had run to the nineteen, and the dropping was the thing he asked for.

In chapter 7, Callum admitted to the council that he had cut the lease page out of the harbor ledger, and he withdrew the marina plan. The admission was the thing he said to the council, and the cutting was the thing he admitted, and the lease page was the thing he had cut, and the harbor ledger was the thing he had cut it from, and the withdrawing was the thing that came after the admission, and the marina plan was the thing he withdrew, and the council was the one before whom he stood and said the thing he had done.

Callum never carried the point of view. Ines and Theo carried the point of view, and Callum was seen only through them, and what Callum thought was shown only through what Ines or Theo saw him do, and the reader never received narration from Callum's head, and Callum was the one who was seen, and Ines and Theo were the ones who saw, and the seeing was the way Callum came to the reader.

Ines and Theo carried the point of view, and Callum and Dot were seen only through them. The point of view was the thing the two of them carried, and Callum and Dot were the ones who were seen through the two of them, and the seeing was the way Callum and Dot came to the reader, and the carrying was the thing Ines and Theo did, and the being seen was the thing Callum and Dot were.
