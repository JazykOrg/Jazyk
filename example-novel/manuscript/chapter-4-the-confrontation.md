# Chapter 4: The confrontation on the quay


The confrontation was the thing the first half of the book had been pointing at, and it was the thing the second half would look back on, and it was the scene that landed both leads at once, the scene that was the midpoint of the whole arc, the scene that stood in the middle of the book the way the quay stood in the middle of the harbor, the line between the water and the town, the line between the two of them.

It was chapter 4, the fourth chapter of the eight, and the fourth chapter was Theo's, because the even chapters belonged to Theo and the odd ones to Ines, and so the confrontation that stood in the middle of the book stood in Theo's head, the head that the book had been building toward since the first chapter opened in Ines's head watching the ferry come in. The midpoint was chapter 4, and chapter 4 was the confrontation on the quay, and the confrontation on the quay was the thing the whole first half had been working toward and the thing the whole second half would work back from.

The quay was the place of it. The quay ran along the top of the harbor wall, the stone wall that had stood for a hundred and fifty years, and the row of shops stood on the quay, and the Lantern Room was one of the shops, and the confrontation was on the quay, on the stone that ran along the top of the wall, the place where the town met the water and the two of them met each other.

Ines came to the quay, and she found Theo there, and she confronted him about the survey, the survey of the wall that Pryce Maritime had hired him for, and she asked him what he had found. The asking was the thing that brought the crack out, the crack he had found in the footing beneath the Lantern Room and that Callum had told him to leave out of the report, and the confrontation was the opening he had been waiting for, the opening he had decided on at low tide when he had stood at the crack and looked up at the room and decided that Ines should know.


The first half of the book had pointed at this scene the way a tide points at the shore. The wall coming down had pointed at it, and the marina that would stand where the wall stood had pointed at it, and the survey that the demolition waited on had pointed at it, and the crack that Theo had found and that Callum had told him to hide had pointed at it, and the lease that Ines had found in the harbor ledger and that tied the frontage to the wall had pointed at it, and the decision Ines had made to take the lease to the council had pointed at it, and all of it, the whole first half, had been the thing that pointed at the confrontation on the quay, the thing that the second half would look back on as the turning point, the scene the book turned on.

The second half looked back on it the way the tide looks back on the shore it has left. The ledger that Ines would present to the council looked back on it, and the vote that the council would take looked back on it, and the crack that Theo would offer as proof that the wall could be repaired looked back on it, and the storm that would come in off the north looked back on it, and the night that Theo would shore up the crack with the harbor crew looked back on it, and the dawn after looked back on it, and the whole second half was the thing that looked back on the confrontation on the quay, the scene that had turned the book and that the book turned back on.

The confrontation landed both leads at once, and that was the thing that made it the midpoint, the scene that changed both of them in the same scene, not one lead and not two disconnected moments but the two of them together, the two of them on the quay, the two of them in the confrontation, the two of them landing at the same time. Ines landed in it, the woman who had come to the quay to ask what he had found, and Theo landed in it, the man who had been waiting for the opening to give her the crack, and the two of them landed together, the two of them in the same scene, the two of them at the same time, and the landing was the thing that made the confrontation the midpoint, the scene that landed both leads at once, the scene the first half pointed at and the second half looked back on.

The confrontation was the middle of the book, and the middle was the thing the book turned on, and the turning was the thing that the two of them did together, the two of them on the quay, the two of them in the scene, the two of them landing at once, and the landing was the midpoint, and the midpoint was chapter 4, and chapter 4 was the confrontation on the quay, and the confrontation on the quay was the scene the first half pointed at and the second half looked back on, and it was the scene that landed both leads at once.
