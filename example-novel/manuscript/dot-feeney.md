# Dot Feeney

Dot Feeney was fifty-eight years old, and she was the ferry office manager. She ran the ferry office on the quay, and she had run it for thirty years, the thirty years that the ferry had crossed the water on the same schedule and the same quay, and the office was hers the way the quay was hers, the way the tide was hers, the way the town was hers.

She had done it for thirty years, and in thirty years she had learned the quay the way a hand learns a tool, and she knew the quay and she knew the ferry and she knew the water, and the ferry office was the place she stood in, and the quay was the place she knew, and the thirty years were the thing that made her the ferry office manager and not only a woman who worked in an office on the quay.

Dot had known Ines since Ines was six. She had called her Nessa since Ines was six, and the name had come to her the way a name comes to a person who has watched a child grow, and Callum had picked the name up at school, the two of them at the same school, and so Callum had called her Nessa too, and the name had passed from Dot to Callum the way a name passes between the people who know a child.

Nobody else used the name. Dot used it, and Callum used it, and no one else in the town used it, and Ines did not answer to it from strangers, because the name was the name of the people who had known her since she was small, and a stranger who called her Nessa was a stranger who did not know her, and Ines did not answer to a name that was not hers from the people who had given it to her.

Dot knew everyone who stepped off the ferry, and she knew everything that happened on the quay. She knew the names of the people who came off the ferry and the names of the people who stayed on the quay, and she knew the things that happened on the quay the way she knew the tide, and the quay was the place she knew, and the ferry was the place she knew, and the two of them were the whole of the town as far as Dot was concerned.

She kept the harbor ledger in the ferry office safe. The ledger was the book that recorded every lease on the quay, and Dot kept it in the safe in the ferry office, and the safe was the place the ledger was, and the ferry office was the place the safe was, and Dot was the one who kept it there, and the ledger was safe because Dot kept it safe.

Dot was the first person in the book to know who Theo worked for, and she told Ines. When Theo came to the ferry office, Dot knew who he was and who he worked for, and she was the first to know it, and she told Ines, and Ines was the one Dot told, because Ines was the one Dot knew, and the telling was the thing Dot did, and the knowing was the thing Dot had.

Dot and Ines knew each other. They had known each other since Ines was six, and Dot was the nearest thing Ines had to family in the town, the nearest thing to a family in a town that was not the family's town, and Dot was the one who knew Ines the way a family knows a child, and Ines was the one Dot knew the way a family knows a child, and the two of them were the nearest thing to family the town had for Ines.

Dot and Theo met when Theo stepped off the ferry. He came to the ferry office, and Dot met him there, and she sent him along the quay to the Lantern Room for a room, and the Lantern Room was the place she sent him, and the quay was the way she sent him, and the room was the thing she sent him for, and Dot was the one who met him and sent him, and Theo was the one who stepped off the ferry and came to the office and was sent along the quay.

In Chapter 3, Dot told Nessa who Theo worked for. It was the scene in which Dot told Ines, who she called Nessa, that Theo worked for Pryce Maritime, and the telling was the thing that happened in the chapter, and Dot was the one who told, and Nessa was the one who was told, and Theo was the one who was named, and Pryce Maritime was the one who was named as the one Theo worked for.

In Chapter 3, Ines asked Dot for the harbor ledger. It was the book in the ferry office safe that recorded every lease on the quay, and Ines asked Dot for it, and Dot was the one who kept it, and the safe was the place it was, and the ferry office was the place the safe was, and the ledger was the thing Ines asked for, and the leases were the thing the ledger recorded, and the quay was the place the leases were for.

When the storm warning came, Dot closed the ferry office and she turned the last ferry back. The storm warning was the thing that came, and Dot was the one who closed the office and turned the ferry back, and the ferry office was the thing she closed, and the last ferry was the thing she turned back, and the quay was the place she did it, and the storm warning was the reason she did it.

Dot never carried the point of view. Ines and Theo carried the point of view, and Dot was seen only through them, and what Dot thought was shown only through what Ines or Theo saw her do, and the reader never received narration from Dot's head, and Dot was the one who was seen, and Ines and Theo were the ones who saw, and the seeing was the way Dot came to the reader.
