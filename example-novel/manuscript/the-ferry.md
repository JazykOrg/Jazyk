# The ferry

The ferry was the only way into Port Alder that did not mean a two-hour drive around the estuary. There was no bridge across the water and no road that ran the length of the shore, and so anyone who came to Port Alder from the mainland came by the ferry, or came by the long road that wound the two hours around the estuary and back. The ferry was the near way, and the drive was the far way, and the town had always been a place you reached by water or by the long road, and the ferry was the thing that made the near way possible.

In autumn the ferry crossed four times a day, and it did not run after dark. The autumn schedule was the short one, the one the town kept when the days grew short and the water grew cold, and it ran four crossings a day, the morning crossing and the noon crossing and the afternoon crossing and the last crossing before the light went, and after the last crossing the ferry did not run, because it did not run after dark, and the town knew the four crossings the way it knew the tides, and the four crossings were the four times a day the water was crossed in autumn.

West coast ferries published their autumn timetables in late August. It was the custom of the coast, the way the ferries on the west coast did it, that the autumn timetables were published in late August, when the summer crowds were thinning and the short days were coming, and the town read the new timetable out of the late August notice, and the late August notice was the thing that told the town when the four crossings would be, and the four crossings would be the autumn schedule, the one that did not run after dark.

When the storm warning came, Dot Feeney turned the last ferry back. Dot ran the ferry office on the quay, and she was the one who kept the harbor ledger safe and who knew the quay and who knew Ines, and when the storm warning came she closed the ferry office and she turned the last ferry back, the last crossing of the day, the one that would have gone out into the water that was about to be the storm, and she turned it back, and the last ferry did not go out, and the last ferry came back to the quay, and the quay was the place the ferry came back to, and Dot was the one who had turned it back.

Chapter 1 opened in Ines's head watching the ferry come in, and chapter 8 closed in Theo's head. The odd chapters belonged to Ines and the even chapters belonged to Theo, and so the first chapter opened in Ines's head, the head that watched the ferry come in across the water, the ferry that was the only way in that did not mean the two-hour drive, and the ferry came in and Ines watched it come in, and the last chapter, the eighth, closed in Theo's head, the head that the book ended in, and the book began in Ines's head watching the ferry and ended in Theo's head, the two heads the book opened and closed on.

Port Alder's setting was the ferry and the quay frontage, the two things the town was built around. The ferry was the way in, the only way in that did not mean the two-hour drive around the estuary, and the quay frontage was the frontage the Marlows held by the Lantern Room lease for as long as the harbor wall stood, and the ferry and the quay frontage were the two setting elements of Port Alder, the two things the town was, the way in and the frontage on the quay, and the town was the ferry and the quay frontage and the wall that held the quay up and the houses on the hill above it.
