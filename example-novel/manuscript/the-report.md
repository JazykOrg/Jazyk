# The report

The written report was the third part of the survey, and it was the part that decided what happened to the wall. Theo wrote it the way every surveyor wrote it, and the way was the thing that made the notebook carry the plot.

Theo did not write the report from memory. He wrote it from the field notebook, the notebook he kept between the walkover and the core samples, and he copied from it the way a copyist copies, line for line, the measurements he had written down by the wall and the readings he had taken from the cores. The report was the notebook set down on paper for the council, not the notebook recalled from his head, and that was why the notebook carried the plot: the report was only as true as the notebook, and the notebook was the thing that could not be faked, the thing that held the crack whether or not the report did.

A written survey report took three days to produce. Theo had the walkover and the core samples by the end of chapter 2, but the report was not a thing that came at once; it took three days to write, three days of copying the notebook and setting the readings down for the council, and so the report could not exist before chapter 3. At the end of chapter 2 the report was not in hand, not finished, not on any table; it was the thing that would come three days later, and the three days were the reason the report was absent from the first half of the book.

In chapter 4, on the quay, Ines asked Theo for the report. She wanted to see it, the written report of the survey, the thing that would tell the council whether the wall could be repaired or had to come down, and she asked him to show it to her. Theo refused. He would not show Ines the report, and he told her to ask Callum, to take her to Callum at Pryce Maritime if she wanted the report, because the report was Callum's to give and not his, and the refusal was the thing that kept the crack out of her hands, the crack that Callum had told him to leave out of the report and that the report, when it came, would not carry.
