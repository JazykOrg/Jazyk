# Manager

A manager is an employee with direct reports. Heads of departments and team leads are
managers.

## Who is a manager

A manager is an employee with direct reports. An employee with at least one direct report
holds the manager role.

Every employee has exactly one manager, not zero and not two.

The head of a department is a manager: the head of a department is an employee with direct
reports.

The lead of a team is a manager: a team's lead holds the manager role, an employee with
direct reports.

The hiring manager is the manager the open role reports to. For a given open role, the
hiring manager is the manager that role reports to.

## Span of control

A manager has at most 8 direct reports. A manager with 8 direct reports is within the
limit; a manager with 9 direct reports exceeds it.

## Reviewing expense claims

The employee submits the expense claim to their manager with a receipt attached to every
line. A claim with a receipt on every line is submitted to the employee's manager; a claim
with a line missing its receipt is not.

The manager reviews the claim and either approves it or returns it to the employee. A
manager reviewing a submitted expense claim either approves it or returns it to the
employee.

An expense claim with a total of 500 dollars or less needs manager approval. An expense
claim with a total of 500 dollars needs the manager's approval.

An expense claim with a total above 500 dollars needs manager approval followed by Finance
approval. An expense claim of 620 dollars is approved by the manager and then by Finance.

When the claim total is above 500 dollars, the manager forwards the approved claim to
Finance for approval. For a claim of 620 dollars, the manager forwards the approved claim
to Finance for approval.

If a receipt is missing, the manager returns the claim to the employee, and the employee
resubmits it with the receipt within 10 business days. When a manager returns an expense
claim for a missing receipt, the employee resubmits it with the receipt within 10 business
days. A resubmission on the 10th business day after the return is within the window; one
on the 11th business day is past it.

If Finance finds a line that is not a business expense, Finance rejects the claim and tells
both the employee and the manager why.

## Spending policy

An expense claim above 250 dollars needs the Finance department's approval in addition to
the manager's approval. An expense claim of 300 dollars needs the manager's approval and
the Finance department's approval.

## Timing

The manager reviews an expense claim within 5 business days of submission. A claim reviewed
on the 5th business day after submission is within the limit; a review on the 6th business
day is late.

A manager answers a time-off request within 2 business days. A manager answers a time-off
request on the 1st business day after the employee submits it. A time-off request answered
on the 2nd business day is within the limit; one answered on the 3rd business day is late.

## Worked example

Dana's manager approves Dana's 620-dollar expense claim on Tuesday.

Dana's manager forwards the approved expense claim to Finance on the same day as approval,
Tuesday.
