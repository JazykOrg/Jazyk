Levels

Every level gets its own diagrams, and digging into an entity shows the level below it

Requirements stay on the entities the documents state. Those entities summarize into higher-level entities, and those into higher still. A level is a node's direct children; the top level is the parentless entities of the project. Each level gets a structural diagram over its members and use case and sequence diagrams over the flows that lift into it. The tree is as deep as the documents and the model's judgment make it: one branch may stop at a service while another goes down to classes.

The pressure that builds the tree is a limit. A node with more than nine direct children opens an optional goal to regroup them, mandatory past fifteen. The harness computes which children belong together, from the requirements that mention them and the relationships between them; the model names the grouping, or declines with a reason when the level is genuinely flat. A grouping is an entity with derived provenance from exactly its members, one sentence of definition, and no quote, because no document states it yet.

Digging in

Three diagrams from one real build, in the order a reader walks them. The project is a bookkeeping backend described in a front door and four short documents, written to cross the children limit twice: twelve concepts at the root, then eleven under Checkout. Every image below is the file the compiler wrote; the boxes link the way the real renderings link to their cards.

1. The top level

The front door names four documents. Their twelve root concepts crossed the limit, and the session grouped them into the areas the documents already name: Orders, Shipping, and Platform. Checkout was a stated process whose own document lists the checkout glossary, so it became the area for that document instead of gaining a duplicate. Ledger, the stated backend, stands beside them.

The top level's sequence draws the flows among its members. Every message here is a sentence stated about leaves two levels down, lifted to the grouping that contains them: Cart and Order are leaves, Checkout and Orders are what the level shows.

view:sequence/checkout-checkout (sequence, 5 steps). The sentence behind the fourth arrow, verbatim from docs/checkout.md: "A Checkout turns a Cart into an Order."

2. The checkout level

One flat glossary stated eleven concepts under Checkout. The session grouped them in seven rounds: Pricing (coupon, discount, tax, promotion), Funds (gift card, loyalty point, payment method), and Selection (cart, wishlist). Address and Receipt stayed where they were, because they share no noun a reader would name. Order and Payment are not children of Checkout; they are drawn because edges from inside the level reach them.

view:class/checkout (class, 7 members): Address, Receipt, Funds, Pricing, Selection, Order, Payment. Click Funds.

The session's justification, as it landed in the journal:

resolved g:abstract-entity:ent:checkout  (7 rounds)
  Checkout's 11 direct children are grouped into three derived groupings
  under it — Pricing (coupon, discount, tax, promotion), Funds (gift-card,
  loyalty-point, payment-method), and Selection (cart, wishlist) — each with
  parent Checkout, derived provenance naming exactly its members, and a
  one-sentence definition, reducing the level to 5 children (≤9). The harness
  files a ratification proposal per derived grouping; Address and Receipt
  stay ungrouped as they share no nameable noun.

The chapter the documents never wrote

A grouping is invented until ratified. No document states Funds, so the compiler proposes the sentence the documents should gain and where it goes: the architecture chapter the documents never wrote. The owner accepts or retracts it. Accepting writes the sentence into the section, and the grouping flips to quoted provenance like any stated entity. Retracting dissolves it: the members return to Checkout, a tombstone redirect keeps the old id resolving, and the next build re-derives whatever the documents still support.

$ jazyk answer g:ratify:ent:funds
diag:ratification-pending-6  ratification-pending on ent:funds
  Should docs/checkout.md state it? "Funds: Funds gathers the balances and
  methods a customer spends at Checkout: the Gift Card, the Loyalty Point,
  and the Payment Method."
  0  Insert into docs/checkout.md /checkout (edit)
  1  Retract (answer)

$ jazyk answer g:ratify:ent:funds --option 0

The reasoning travels with the proposal, so the owner reads why the model chose the name: the documents name a payment cluster of the gift card, the loyalty point, and the payment method; "Payment" was taken by a different concept, the one that settles an Invoice; so the area is named Funds, and the proposal is stated as a choice. A split the documents never suggest is always a choice, and the proposal carries it to the owner instead of deciding it.

One walk, three surfaces

The cards and the diagram pages are plain markdown on disk, one file per entity and one per diagram, rewritten on every commit and pruned with their node. A reader with no server opens a card in a markdown preview and walks. The same model serves two more places:

On disk

A card per entity, a page per diagram with a clickable legend, a page per level with its members. The index opens with the top level's members.

In the editor

Hovering an entity in a document shows the card in short: the definition, the breadcrumb, the level it sits in, and the link inside. Go to type definition opens the card.

In the GUI

The containment tree, a breadcrumb over the diagram panel, the diagrams inline and clickable, history behind an addressable URL, and more detail expanding a grouping in place.

Diagrams stay projections. A level view derives from the tree and the requirements on its leaves; deleting a rendering loses nothing, and a reparent redraws every level it touches on the next commit.

Measured on this example: a front door and four documents, two fan-outs (twelve at the root, eleven under Checkout), a tree four levels deep with the shape 5 / 17 / 9 / 1, every level under nine children, about ten sessions on a local 27B model. The full record is in bootstrap/VALIDATION.md; the design is in docs/compiler/concepts/levels.md.
